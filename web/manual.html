Cluster Conquerors of SPLORR!!

The manual. Everything in writing, as the Homeowners' Association would want.

A Metaphor about paperwork, in the shape of a turn-based space strategy game in the spirit of the original Master of Orion. You are one species of animal in a small cluster of about twenty stars, with two rival species. You survey the stars, settle them, run each colony, research hobbies, build ships, and fight. Everybody hates you. The rivals hate each other too, but you get it worst.

The Metaphor is still being built. This is slice 2 of 3. Bylaws, the President's rulings, random events, bigger clusters and more rivals are still to come. This manual describes only what is in it today.

Yer first ten turns

  1. Pick a species. Arrows to move, Enter to choose (or tap twice). Each has one trait; the table below says what. If you cannot decide, the Beavers (more from factories) are the plainest start.
  2. Look around. You start at yer home star, ringed in green. Almost every other star is grey: unknown. The panel on the right always describes the star under the cursor. The news runs along the bottom.
  3. Send yer two Inspectors out. Press Enter on home to open the colony, pick Ships parked here >, then:
    • Up/Down picks a kind of ship (the Inspector). Left/Right sets how many go: start with one.
    • Pick Choose where, move the cursor to a grey star, and press Enter (or tap the star twice). The dotted line is cyan when the trip can be made, and red when it cannot. The panel shows the turns the trip takes and the fuel left on arrival.
    • Do it again with the other Inspector, towards a different star.
  4. End the turn with T. An Inspector files a survey of each star it reaches. Surveyed stars turn yellow and show their planet in the panel.
  5. Settle with the Moving Van. Once a survey shows a good world nearby (Garden, Terran, Ocean or Jungle; Medium or Large), send the Moving Van there. It settles on arrival with 2 households and is used up. Hostile worlds (Barren, Dead, Inferno, Toxic, Radiated) need a Landscaping tech first, unless you are Tardigrades.
  6. Set yer home's chores. Open the colony (Enter on home). The five sliders share out what it makes. Early on, keep Home Improvement high to build factories, keep a little Lawn Care so the yards stay tidy, and give Book Club a share for research. Set the ship order to a Moving Van and raise Parking when you want another colony.
  7. Research. R opens the research screen. Its six sliders share yer Book Club points between six hobbies. Landscaping (more worlds to settle) and Commuting (speed and range) pay off early.
  8. Keep ending turns. Around turn 10 you will probably meet a rival. They hate you. Keep a few Golf Carts at home, and build Fencing (defence bases) at colonies near them.

The screen and the map

The map is on the left and scrolls with the cursor. The panel is on the right: turn number, yer BJ (billion jools, the money), and whatever the cursor is on. The news runs along the bottom, newest last.

On the mapMeans
Grey starUnknown: never surveyed, or its survey was misfiled.
Yellow starSurveyed. The panel shows what the report says, which may be wrong.
Green ringOne of yer colonies.
Red or orange ringA rival's colony, shown only while in sight.
Small flagYer ships parked at a star. A red flag means stranded (out of fuel). A rival's ships show as a flag at the top left, in its colour.
A van between starsA fleet on its way, in its owner's colour.
Purple vortexA wormhole you have been through.

You see about 7 cells around each of yer colonies and ships, and everything an ally sees. Beyond that, rivals are invisible.

A turn happens when you end it. Everyone's colonies produce, research is spent, fleets move and arrive, fuel burns, battles are found, and now and then the Homeowners' Association meets. Yer battles, a meeting and any forms on yer doormat then come up one after another before you are back on the map.

Colonies and the five chores

A colony has households (its people), factories, defence bases and waste. Each turn it makes production: half a point per household, plus a point per factory the households can work (two factories per household; three for Ants). Rich worlds make half as much again, and Poor worlds half as much.

A tenth of production goes to yer BJ as tax. The five chore sliders share the rest, always adding up to 100. Moving one shares the difference among the others that are not locked.

ChoreDoes
ParkingBuilds the colony's ship order. Ships appear at the colony.
FencingBuilds defence bases at 30 points each. They fight in battles at the colony and stop landings.
Home ImprovementBuilds factories at 10 points each (less with Renovation techs), up to what the households can work. Once that is reached, the surplus goes to BJ.
Lawn CareCleans waste. Every 4 factories make 1 waste a turn, and a point cleans 2. Every 10 waste left lying about permanently takes room for one household off the planet, with a complaint in the news. Spare Lawn Care helps households grow.
Book ClubMakes research points, doubled on Ancient Artifacts worlds.

Room: a Small world holds up to 40 households, Medium 70 and Large 100, times the type: Garden 125 %, Terran 100 %, Ocean and Jungle 90 %, Arid and Steppe 80 %, Desert and Tundra 70 %. Hostile types hold less, once you can settle them at all. Households grow towards the room each turn, slower as it fills.

Home starts with 50 households and 30 factories, building Inspectors.

Sending: Enter on a star with yer ships (or Ships parked here > on a colony) opens the list. Pick kinds and counts, then Choose where, then the star. Esc steps back.

Fuel and Gas Cans

A fleet counts the turns it spends away from one of yer colonies (or an ally's). Its range is the fuel of its thirstiest ship. An order that would run dry on the way is refused ("Out of range."). A fleet that runs dry anywhere that is not yer colony is stranded (red flag) and waits there forever, until a Gas Can reaches it.

A Gas Can fills up with 10 refills at yer colonies (or an ally's). Away from home, it tops its own fleet up with one refill a turn. So send Gas Cans along on long trips, or send one to rescue a stranded fleet. Commuting techs add speed and fuel.

Surveys, and how they lie

  • Wrong readings: about 15 % of reports get the planet's type or size wrong. The truth comes out when a Moving Van arrives. If it turns out the world cannot be settled, the movers wait in the van.
  • Estate-agent language: a star with no special is sometimes reported Rich or Ancient Artifacts. A new colony finds out after 3 turns. Only flattering lies are told.
  • Misfiling: each turn, each report (not home's) has a 1 % chance of being lost, and the star shows grey again.
  • Wormholes: about one star in eight is a wormhole that looks exactly like an unknown star. Anything that enters is thrown to a random star. After that you know it, and it is drawn as a purple vortex.

Spreadsheets techs make bad readings rarer.

Research

Book Club points go into a pool that six empire-wide sliders share between six hobbies. Each hobby buys its cheapest available tech when it has enough. Each cluster offers only 5 of each hobby's 8 techs, so nobody gets everything.

HobbyGives
SpreadsheetsShips aim better; fewer bad survey readings
RenovationCheaper factories; ship armour
FencesShip shields; stronger defence bases
LandscapingSettle hostile worlds (Barren, Dead, Inferno, Toxic, Radiated, one tech each); more room; Composting halves waste
CommutingShip speed and fuel
DisputesShip weapons (more damage per hit)

The upgrade fee: when a ship tech arrives, every affected ship upgrades at once and you are billed 5 BJ per ship. A fleet you cannot pay for keeps its old kit and is marked Unpaid in orange. Pay later with Pay upgrades in its ship list. Unpaid fees are also a complaint at the Homeowners' Association.

Battles

At the end of a turn, two empires at the same star fight if one of them can shoot (armed ships or defence bases) and the other has something to shoot at. Yer battles open on a 10 by 8 grid. Each side's ships are stacks of one type, warships in front and bases at the back.

  • Left/Right picks the enemy stack to aim at (red brackets). Up/Down picks an action: Fight a round, Fight it out (the rest of the battle at once, with the same rolls) or Retreat (to yer nearest colony in reach).
  • The dice are hidden. A shot hits 50 % of the time, plus 10 per aiming tech (Geese +10, and a fifth less against Tortoises). A hit does 3 damage plus weapons, less the target's shields, and always at least 1.
  • A battle still undecided after 10 rounds is broken off, and both sides stay (and fight again next turn). Ships are repaired after every battle.
  • The news gives only the losses each round.

Battles between the rivals are fought out at once and appear in the news only if you can see them.

Taking colonies

A Carpool takes 5 households from the colony that builds it (a colony with 5 or fewer waits). Carpools parked at an enemy colony that has no defence bases and no armed ships left land at the start of the next turn.

A quarter of the colony's households fight as militia, one against one with hidden rolls, at even odds (65 % for Badgers landing, 35 % against Badger households). Win, and the colony is yers with its factories. Its people keep their species and that species' trait. They complain at every meeting of the Homeowners' Association until they settle in after 20 turns.

The Clubhouse and its moth

Somewhere far from every home is The Clubhouse: a Large Garden world with Ancient Artifacts. Its porch light is on, and it has drawn a very large space moth. The moth has 72 hits and 12 shots at range 4, never moves, and heals completely between battles. Any ships that park there fight it. Nobody can settle there while it lives.

Once the moth is gone, the first Moving Van to settle the Clubhouse finds three lost techs, from any hobby. Bring a lot of Pickups, or a few Monster Trucks.

The Homeowners' Association

Once every empire has met every other one, the Association meets, and then every 25 turns after that. The minutes come up on screen after the turn.

  • Complaints: each captured colony whose people have not settled in complains. Each empire also gets at most one more complaint: yards in a mess (5 or more waste at a colony), ships parked at someone else's colony (an ally's is fine), or unpaid upgrade fees. Frame-ups that fooled nobody are read out as complaints too (see the Watch). Each complaint fines 5 % of yer BJ.
  • The vote is held at the first meeting, the third, and every other one after. The two empires with the most households stand. Votes follow households, and a winner needs two thirds of all of them. You vote for a candidate (yerselves, if standing) or abstain. The rivals never vote for you.
  • Being President counts as a win, but play goes on, and every later meeting costs the President 10 BJ for the biscuits.

Grudges and paperwork

Grudges

Every empire keeps a hidden grudge against every other one, from 0 to 100. The rivals start at 60 against you and 30 against each other (20 less against Dogs). Battles, landings, captured colonies, threats, fines and watchers caught all add to it. It cools by a point a turn, but never below where it started. You never see the numbers; the news passes on gossip when a rival's feelings change ("have stopped waving", "have started a petition about you", "have written yer name on their bins"). The rivals send their fleets at whoever they hate most among the targets they can beat.

The forms desk

P opens it. Pick a rival you have met (Left/Right on To), pick a row, change its figure or tech with Left/Right, and file it with Enter. The answer comes in the news. Below the forms, the desk shows yer treaty and trade with that rival, the last gossip, and what is in the post.

With a pact, neither side's ships fight the other's and nobody lands Carpools. A refusal adds a little grudge.

"Strong" means warship hits plus defence bases. The Association keeps a register, so everyone knows everyone's. A rival tears up everything itself when its grudge reaches 80, or when it becomes twice as strong as you. Since their grudge against you never cools below 60, most deals need some tribute first.

Yer doormat

Now and then a rival who has met you files a form at you. When it is the stronger, it sends a demand for some of yer BJ. When it would rather be left alone, it sends a pact, already signed; once you have a pact, it may send a trade agreement. Pay or sign, or refuse. Refusing a demand adds 15 to its grudge.

The Neighbourhood Watch

Yer spies. W opens the Watch screen.

  • Budget: Left/Right sends 0 to 25 % of yer BJ income to the Watch, in steps of 5. Every 20 BJ saved trains a watcher. The Watch holds at most 20.
  • At home, watchers look out for the other side's. A rival's watchers are more likely to be caught, and less likely to get anything done, while you keep some at home.
  • On a rival: on that rival's watchers row, Right sends a watcher from home and Left calls one back. The mission row below picks what they do: just watch, steal techs, wreck factories (5 at a time), wreck bases or wreck ships (one at a time).
  • Every turn, each watcher on a rival lets you see that rival's colonies and parked ships. Each one on a mission has 10 % to succeed, plus 5 for every watcher you have there beyond the ones they keep at home (at most 60 %). At most one success per rival per turn.
  • Getting caught: a watcher on a mission is caught 20 % of the time, one just watching 5 %, and more when they have watchers at home. A caught watcher is gone, and the rival hates you 10 more. When their watchers are caught, the news tells you.
  • Raccoons succeed half as often again and are caught half as often.

When someone steals from you or wrecks something of yers, the news says what. It never says who. The rivals keep a Watch too: a tenth of their income, one watcher at home, the rest on whoever they hate most.

Frame-ups

Below each rival's mission is a frame-up row. Left/Right names the other rival (once you have met them) or nobody. A frame-up only counts on a mission; watchers who just watch leave no evidence. With a frame-up on, every time yer watchers there are caught or get something done, the victim finds the other rival's name all over it:

  • Three times in four it works. The victim's grudge for that lands on the rival you named: 10 for each watcher caught, and 10 for a mission done (which, without a frame-up, nobody finds out about). The news tells you they blame the other one.
  • Otherwise it fools nobody. The victim hates you twice what it would have, and the Association reads it out at the next meeting and fines you for it, like any other complaint.

The rivals frame too: they name the third empire (you, as often as not) unless they have a pact with it. A frame-up that works fools you as well: the news names whoever was framed, and you are told otherwise only when it was done badly. At most three frame-ups are read out at a meeting, and yers come first.

Winning, losing and saves

  • You win when no rival has a colony left.
  • You lose when you have none.
  • Being elected President of the Homeowners' Association also counts as a win, and play goes on.

An empire with no colonies is out, and its ships vanish. Enter on the end screen starts a new cluster.

Saves are automatic. The cluster is kept in yer browser after every move and picked up when you come back. New cluster under the screen starts afresh. Adding ?seed=123 to the address plays a particular cluster. A save from an older build is ignored and a new cluster starts.

Tips

  • Survey before you settle. A good-looking report is wrong about one time in seven.
  • Watch the yards. Waste costs room for households forever, and it is a fine at every meeting. Composting (Landscaping) halves it.
  • Keep some BJ in hand to pay upgrade fees, or yer ships keep their old kit.
  • Defence bases stop landings. A colony with even one base, or one armed ship, cannot be invaded.
  • Range is the clock. Check the fuel shown in the panel before sending anything far, and take a Gas Can on long trips.
  • Keep a watcher or two at home. The rivals spy on whoever they hate most, and that is you.
  • Tribute buys time, not friends. It lowers a grudge only so far, and the rivals still hate you.
  • The Clubhouse is worth it, if you bring enough to beat a 72-hit moth that heals between visits.
